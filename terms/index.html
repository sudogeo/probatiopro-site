3. What the app does, and what it does not claim

The app reads your permission schemes, project roles and group membership, asks Jira's own effective permission check what each account can do, reports where the two disagree, and records the decisions your reviewers make. Each review is sealed as a dated snapshot with a content hash that a recipient can recompute from the exported file alone.

It does not certify anything about your organisation. It produces a record of a review that your own people performed, from a configuration that your own people maintain. Three consequences worth stating plainly:

  • The review is only as good as its inputs. If a group is misnamed, a project is misconfigured, or a reviewer approves without looking, the app records that faithfully. It reports what it can see; it does not supply judgement.
  • It is not legal, audit or compliance advice. The app maps its findings to published framework references so your team can work with them. Whether your programme satisfies DORA, NIS2, ISO/IEC 27001, SOC 2, PCI DSS or any other obligation is a determination for you, your auditor and your advisers — never for us, and never for a tool.
  • Some limits are the platform's. Facts Jira does not expose to an app cannot be shown by the app. Where that affects a review, the app says so on the screen and in the export rather than leaving a silence.
